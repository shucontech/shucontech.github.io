How this bed turnover rate calculator works

Bed turnover rate tells you how many patients moved through each available bed in a period. Bed turnover interval tells you how long, on average, a bed stood empty between one patient leaving and the next being admitted. Together they show how well beds, discharges, housekeeping and admissions are working as one flow.

Bed turnover rate = Total discharges in the period ÷ Average available beds in the period
Bed turnover interval = (Available bed days − Occupied bed days) ÷ Total discharges

Available bed days are average available beds × days in the period. Occupied bed days are the sum of the daily midnight census. Use the same discharge definition every month; this calculator includes deaths, as many hospitals count all separations from inpatient care, but document whichever definition you use.

Bed turnover rate formula in hospital: worked example

A 100-bed hospital has 5 beds closed for maintenance, so it averages 95 available beds in a 31-day month. It records 420 discharges and 2,210 occupied bed days.

Bed turnover rate vs bed occupancy rate

Occupancy asks how full the beds were; turnover asks how many patients moved through each bed. Read them together:

  • High occupancy, low turnover: long stays, complex cases or discharge delays
  • Low occupancy, high turnover: short-stay cases but weak demand or capacity mismatch
  • High occupancy, high turnover: strong demand and active patient flow; watch staff pressure
  • Low occupancy, low turnover: a demand, referral or service-line issue

What is a good bed turnover rate?

There is no universal good bed turnover rate. It depends on specialty mix, case complexity, local demand, payer mix and bed category: a day-care-heavy surgical unit will turn beds faster than a medical ICU. Compare against your own three-month and twelve-month trend, set expectations by department, and discuss the outliers. Reducing empty-bed time creates value only when there is patient demand and safe staffing, so never push turnover faster than clinical readiness allows.

Frequently asked questions

What is bed turnover rate in a hospital?

Bed turnover rate is the number of discharged patients each available bed served during a period. It shows how actively beds are being used for new admissions.

What is the bed turnover rate formula?

Bed turnover rate = Total discharges during the period ÷ Average available beds during the period. Use the same discharge definition, including deaths if that is your policy, every month.

How do you use the bed turnover rate formula in hospital reporting?

Take the month’s discharges and divide by the average beds that were actually available. For example, 420 discharges with 95 average available beds gives 420 ÷ 95 = 4.42, so each bed served about 4.4 patients that month.

What is bed turnover interval?

Bed turnover interval is the average time a bed stays empty between one patient leaving and the next being admitted. Bed turnover interval = (Available bed days − Occupied bed days) ÷ Total discharges. For example, (2,945 − 2,210) ÷ 420 = 1.75 days, or about 42 hours.

What is the difference between bed turnover rate and bed occupancy rate?

Occupancy shows how full beds were; turnover shows how many patients moved through each bed. A hospital can have high occupancy with low turnover when stays are long, or moderate occupancy with high turnover when short-stay cases dominate.

What is a good bed turnover rate?

There is no universal good bed turnover rate. It depends on specialty mix, case complexity, demand and bed category, so compare against your own trend and set expectations by department.
