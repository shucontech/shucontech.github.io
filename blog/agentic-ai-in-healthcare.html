Agentic AI in healthcare is an approach in which AI systems work towards a defined goal by planning steps, using hospital software and data, taking permitted actions and escalating to people when needed. Where generative AI answers a prompt, agentic AI is built to finish a piece of work, such as getting a claim file ready or closing a discharge, under human supervision. The software workers that do this are called AI agents in healthcare, and our main guide covers their types and use cases.

This article explains the idea, what it looks like inside an Indian hospital, how much autonomy to allow and how to tell a real agentic product from a rebranded chatbot.

What agentic AI means in healthcare

The word agentic comes from agency: the ability to act towards a goal. Traditional hospital software waits for a user to click. A generative AI assistant waits for a prompt. An agentic system is given an outcome and a set of boundaries, and it works out the steps in between.

Take a cashless claim that is stuck. A generative AI tool can draft a reply to the insurer's query if someone pastes it in. An agentic system starts from the goal that every pending claim keeps moving. It checks the worklist, notices the query, confirms the lab report it needs is in the file, drafts the reply, sends it to the TPA executive for approval and checks the claim again the next morning. Nobody has to ask it each time.

Four properties separate agentic AI from the tools hospitals already know:

  • Goal-directed: it is given an outcome to reach, not a single question to answer.
  • Multi-step: it plans a sequence of actions and adjusts when a step fails.
  • Connected: it reads from and writes to real systems such as the HMS, billing, pharmacy and telephony.
  • Bounded: it works inside permissions, approval rules and escalation paths the hospital defines.

How agentic a system should be depends on the risk of the task, which is why the autonomy levels below matter more than the label.

Generative AI vs agentic AI vs AI agents

The three terms are used loosely, often in the same sales presentation. They describe different things.

What starts it?A person's promptA goal, an event or a scheduleA task assigned by the system or a person

The simplest way to hold them apart: generative AI is the skill, agentic AI is the way of working, and AI agents are the workers. An agentic system usually has generative AI inside it but adds planning, tools, memory and controls around it. For a comparison with older automation, see AI agents vs RPA and chatbots.

How an agentic AI system works

An agentic system has a small number of parts.

Agentic AI = goal + plan + tools + memory + guardrails + human escalation
  • Goal: a specific outcome set by the hospital, for example that no claim query stays unanswered for more than 24 hours.
  • Plan: the system breaks the goal into steps and re-plans when something changes.
  • Tools: the approved actions it can take, such as reading a bill, placing a call, creating a task or sending a draft for approval.
  • Memory: a record of what has been done on each case, so a file can be picked up the next day.
  • Guardrails: permissions and limits that define which data the system may read, what it must never do and when it must stop.
  • Human escalation: named people who receive approvals and exceptions, with enough context to decide quickly.

Larger deployments add orchestration: several specialised agents each handle one job and a coordinating layer passes work between them. A discharge may involve one agent watching billing, one watching pharmacy returns and one tracking the TPA approval, with orchestration deciding who acts next.

Agentic AI examples in Indian hospitals

The examples below are written as workflows, because agentic AI should be judged by the work it closes. The figures are illustrative, not benchmarks, and more ideas are in our list of AI use cases in hospital operations.

Claims desk

Goal: every insured file is complete before submission and no payer query waits. The system reviews the pending list every hour, checks each file against that payer's document checklist, flags a missing investigation report to the ward, drafts query replies for the TPA executive to approve and raises aged files to the insurance head. In a hospital submitting, say, 600 files a month, the desk spends its mornings clearing pending work instead of finding it.

Discharge coordination

Goal: a patient marked fit for discharge leaves within the hospital's target time. The system watches for the discharge order, then tracks the summary, pharmacy returns, pending charges, the final bill and the insurer's approval in parallel. When one item stalls, it reminds the owner and, after a defined wait, escalates to the floor manager. It never prepares the bill or signs the summary.

Pharmacy reconciliation

Goal: every item dispensed to an inpatient is either billed or returned. The system compares prescriptions, dispensing records, returns and the running bill each day and sends the mismatches, with the likely reason, to the pharmacy in-charge and billing supervisor before discharge. If consumables worth a hypothetical ₹8,000 are missing from one bill, the correction happens while the patient is still admitted, not in an audit three months later.

Patient follow-up calls

Goal: every discharged patient gets a follow-up call on the scheduled day. An AI voice agent for hospitals places the call, asks the scripted questions, books a review slot if needed, retries unreachable numbers and hands over to a nurse or doctor as soon as a red-flag answer comes up. Staff call back only the patients who need a person.

Levels of autonomy for hospital work

The most useful question about any agentic system is not how intelligent it is but what it is allowed to do without asking. Hospitals can think in four levels.

LevelWhat the system doesHospital exampleWhat a human still does
1. AssistDrafts or summarises when askedDrafts a reply to a TPA queryReads, corrects and sends it
2. RecommendMonitors data and suggests the next actionFlags an unbilled OT consumable before dischargeDecides whether to add the charge
3. Act with approvalPrepares the action and waits for sign-offAssembles a claim file and queues it for the TPA executiveApproves, edits or rejects each step
4. Act within limitsCompletes low-risk steps on its own inside set limitsPlaces reminder calls and books follow-up slotsSets the limits, handles exceptions and audits samples

Autonomy is a dial, not a switch

Set the level for each task, not for the whole product. The same system can act within limits on reminder calls and stay at recommend for anything that touches a bill, a discount or a clinical decision.

Clinical judgement, financial approvals and legal declarations stay with people at every level. This is the principle of human-in-the-loop AI in healthcare: the software chases and checks, and a named person owns the decision.

Why many agentic AI projects fail

Gartner predicts that over 40% of agentic AI projects will be cancelled by the end of 2027 because of escalating costs, unclear business value or inadequate risk controls. The same release expects the technology to keep spreading: by 2028, at least 15% of day-to-day work decisions will be made autonomously through agentic AI, and 33% of enterprise software applications will include agentic AI, up from less than 1% in 2024.

Both things can be true: the technology works, and many projects are still badly chosen. In India, a report by Bain & Company with HealthQuad finds that AI in Indian healthcare remains largely assistive today, and that the main barriers to scale are data quality, workflow integration, clinician trust and change management. None of those is a problem with the AI model itself.

In hospitals, agentic projects usually fail for ordinary reasons:

  • No single owner is accountable for the workflow.
  • Nobody measured the baseline, so the benefit cannot be shown.
  • Billing, pharmacy and claims data sit in separate systems that do not agree.
  • The system can only read data, so staff still do every action by hand.
  • Running costs were never compared with the value recovered.

How to spot agent washing

Gartner also warns about "agent washing": vendors rebranding existing assistants, RPA tools and chatbots as agents without real agentic capability. Six requests in a demo are enough to test for it.

  1. Show an action, not an answer. Ask the vendor to complete a real step, such as updating a claim status, not just reply in a chat window.
  2. Show what happens tomorrow. Ask what the system does, unprompted, with a file that is still pending the next day.
  3. Show the systems it connects to. Ask which of your systems it reads from and writes to, with your HMS named.
  4. Show the approval points. Ask where limits and sign-offs are configured and who in your team can change them.
  5. Show the audit log. Every action should be traceable to a time, a data source and an approver.
  6. Show the metric. Ask which number it will move in a pilot, such as query ageing or discharge time, and how that will be measured.

A product that passes only the first test is a chatbot with a new name. One that fails the last has no business case, which is the more expensive mistake.

Governance for agentic AI in India

Because agentic systems act, governance matters more than it does for a tool that only drafts text. The ICMR Ethical Guidelines for Application of Artificial Intelligence in Biomedical Research and Healthcare (2023) set out principles that include accountability and liability, human oversight, data privacy, and risk minimisation and safety.

On privacy, hospitals should plan for the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023. The DPDP Rules were notified in November 2025 and their obligations are being phased in, so confirm with legal counsel which requirements apply to your hospital and from when. In practice, good governance comes down to a few habits:

  • A named business owner for every agentic workflow.
  • Role-based access, with the system reading only the data the task needs.
  • Consent-aware patient communication and an easy route to a human.
  • Logs of every action, reviewed in a regular meeting.
  • A clear way to pause the system when something looks wrong.

How to start with agentic AI in a hospital

  1. Pick one workflow. Choose administrative work with high volume, clear rules and a visible cost of delay, such as claim queries or follow-up calls.
  2. Record the baseline. Note volumes, turnaround time, staff hours and rupees at stake before switching anything on.
  3. Start at a low autonomy level. Begin at recommend or act with approval.
  4. Write the approval rules. Decide which steps are automatic, which need sign-off and which always go to a person.
  5. Run a time-boxed pilot. Four to eight weeks with a weekly review is usually enough to judge.
  6. Widen carefully. Raise autonomy or add the next workflow only after the first shows a measured gain.

Frequently asked questions

What is agentic AI in healthcare?

Agentic AI in healthcare is an approach in which AI systems work towards a defined goal, such as a claim file that is ready to submit, by planning steps, using hospital software and escalating to staff. It is designed to complete work under supervision, not only to answer questions.

What is the difference between generative AI and agentic AI in healthcare?

Generative AI produces content, such as a draft discharge summary or a reply to a payer query, when a person asks for it. Agentic AI uses that capability inside a workflow: it decides the next step, uses tools, checks the result and follows up until the task is closed or handed to a human.

Is agentic AI the same as AI agents?

Not exactly. Agentic AI is the approach or system design, and AI agents are the individual software workers inside it, each with a specific job, permissions and escalation rules.

What are examples of agentic AI in hospitals?

Common examples are claim file readiness and payer query follow-up, discharge coordination across billing, pharmacy and the TPA desk, pharmacy reconciliation against billing, and patient follow-up calls with handover to staff.

Is agentic AI safe to use in hospitals?

It can be when autonomy is matched to risk. Hospitals should limit permissions, keep audit logs, require human approval for clinical, financial and legal decisions, and review the system's actions regularly.

How should a hospital start with agentic AI?

Start with one administrative workflow that has a clear owner and a measurable outcome, such as claim query ageing or missed follow-up calls. Run it at a low autonomy level first and widen permissions only after results and errors have been reviewed.
