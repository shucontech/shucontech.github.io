The pre-authorization process in a hospital is the workflow for getting provisional approval from an insurer or TPA before treatment is billed as cashless. It usually confirms eligibility, diagnosis, estimated cost, planned treatment and document completeness before the patient can rely on insurer payment. In practice, pre-authorisation is not just an insurance desk activity; it depends on the admitting doctor, front office, billing team, patient family and payer all sharing accurate information quickly.

What pre-authorisation means in a hospital

Pre-authorisation, often called pre-auth, is a request sent by the hospital to the insurer or third-party administrator for cashless approval. The payer reviews whether the patient has an active policy, whether the proposed treatment appears covered and whether the estimated amount is reasonable under the policy terms. The approval may be partial, conditional or subject to final scrutiny after discharge.

Hospitals should explain this clearly to patients. A pre-auth approval is not the same as a final settlement guarantee. Non-covered items, co-pay, room rent restrictions, sub-limits, consumables and documentation issues can still create patient-payable amounts. Because payer formats and requirements differ, hospitals should check current official and payer-specific guidance rather than relying on an old checklist.

Pre-authorization process in a hospital: step by step

The workflow starts when a cashless patient is identified at admission or before a planned admission. First, the hospital verifies policy details, card information, patient identity and payer network status. Second, the doctor documents diagnosis, planned treatment and expected stay. Third, billing prepares an estimate by room category, procedure, pharmacy and investigations. Fourth, the insurance desk submits the pre-auth request with supporting documents. Fifth, the payer approves, rejects or raises a query.

  1. Register the patient and choose cashless payer type.
  2. Collect policy card, ID proof and any employer or corporate documents.
  3. Get admission advice, diagnosis and initial treatment plan from the doctor.
  4. Prepare a realistic estimate with room category and expected consumables.
  5. Submit pre-auth form and documents through the payer channel.
  6. Track queries, replies, approval amount and conditions.

This is the front half of the broader hospital billing process teams manage until final discharge approval.

Documents and checklist for cashless pre-auth

A complete file reduces avoidable back-and-forth. Hospitals should maintain payer-wise checklists, but a general Indian hospital checklist includes identity proof, policy number or health card, admission advice, provisional diagnosis, clinical notes, investigation reports, estimate, expected length of stay and signed forms. For accident, maternity, planned surgery or chronic disease cases, payers may ask for additional documents.

Keep the checklist payer-wise

A single generic checklist is useful for training, but the live desk should maintain payer-wise document requirements and update them when a TPA changes its portal or format.

Document quality matters as much as document presence. A handwritten diagnosis that cannot be read, an estimate without implants, or a report uploaded under the wrong patient can all delay approval. Hospitals can reduce queries by using standard admission templates and mandatory fields for diagnosis, treatment plan and estimated cost.

Worked INR example for pre-authorisation

Suppose a patient is admitted for a planned laparoscopic procedure. The hospital estimates room rent at ₹24,000 for three days, surgeon and OT charges at ₹70,000, anaesthesia at ₹18,000, diagnostics at ₹12,000, pharmacy and consumables at ₹28,000 and other services at ₹8,000. The total estimate is ₹1,60,000. The hospital submits a pre-auth request for ₹1,60,000 with reports and doctor's notes.

Common TPA queries and how to prevent them

Common queries, including TPA claim rejection reasons, include incomplete diagnosis, missing first consultation note, unclear injury history, mismatch between policyholder and patient, room category conflict, estimate not matching planned procedure, missing investigation report and insufficient justification for admission. These causes overlap with claim deduction patterns, so the prevention mindset should begin at pre-auth stage.

  • For identity mismatch: verify spelling, age and relation at registration.
  • For clinical queries: ask doctors for short, specific treatment justification.
  • For estimate queries: break down procedure, room, pharmacy and consumables.
  • For document gaps: assign one owner to collect and upload the missing item.
  • For policy limits: communicate likely patient-payable amounts early.

Hospitals should also track query ageing. A query sitting with the ward for four hours can become a discharge delay the next day. A simple dashboard of payer, patient, query reason, owner and time pending can prevent many escalations.

Planned admission versus emergency admission

In a planned admission, the best practice is to start the pre-auth file before the patient reaches the hospital. Reports, diagnosis, planned procedure and estimate can be checked calmly, and the family can be told what is likely to be cashless and what may remain payable. This avoids the common situation where the patient arrives ready for admission but the insurance desk is still discovering policy limits.

Emergency admissions are different. Treatment cannot wait for a perfect file, so hospitals need a rapid minimum checklist followed by a second-stage completion process. The emergency team should capture identity, policy information if available, clinical note, provisional diagnosis and admission reason. Once the patient is stable, the insurance desk can complete the estimate, upload reports and chase missing documents from relatives. This staged method keeps care moving without losing claim discipline.

What to communicate to patients

Families often hear the word cashless and assume nothing will be payable. The insurance desk should explain that approval can be partial, sub-limits may apply, and final settlement depends on policy conditions and documents. This explanation should happen early and be documented. A short, respectful conversation at admission is better than a difficult argument after the discharge bill is ready.

Pre-auth controls for finance and operations

Pre-auth affects cash flow, patient experience and claim quality; it should connect with the cashless claim process. Finance should review approval gaps for high-value cases daily. Operations should measure time from admission to request submission, query turnaround time and approvals pending beyond expected TAT. Claims teams should compare pre-auth estimates with final bill differences to improve future estimates.

Useful controls include a mandatory pre-auth checklist, payer-wise templates, daily pending report, escalation rules for queries, estimate variance review and final approval reconciliation. If the hospital also measures claim deductions in hospital bills, pre-auth query reasons should feed into the same quality loop.

Pre-auth teams should also mark possible deduction risks early. If an item is commonly excluded, if a package boundary is unclear, or if the approved room category differs from the occupied room, the file should be flagged before discharge. This connects pre-auth discipline with claim deductions in hospital bills and helps the hospital avoid surprises during final settlement.

Connecting pre-auth to final claim settlement

The final claim is only as good as the pre-auth foundation. If diagnosis, package, implant usage or room category is unclear at the start, final approval becomes harder. During discharge, the final bill, discharge summary, prescriptions, reports and receipts should align with the pre-auth trail. Any clinical change should be documented when it happens, not explained after the payer asks.

Frequently asked questions

What is pre-authorisation in cashless hospitalisation?

Pre-authorisation is the insurer or TPA's provisional approval for cashless treatment based on the patient's policy, diagnosis, estimated cost and submitted documents.

Is pre-authorisation a guarantee of full payment?

No. It is a provisional approval. The final payable amount can change after document review, policy conditions, exclusions, co-pay, room rent limits and final bill checks.

Which documents are needed for hospital pre-authorisation?

Common documents include policy or card details, ID proof, admission advice, diagnosis, estimate, investigation reports and the treating doctor's notes. Current payer requirements should always be checked.

Why does a TPA raise queries during pre-authorisation?

Queries are usually raised for missing documents, unclear diagnosis, mismatch in policy details, estimate differences, room category issues or insufficient clinical justification.

How can hospitals speed up pre-authorisation?

Use standard document checklists, capture clinical justification clearly, submit complete estimates, track query ownership and monitor ageing by payer and department.
